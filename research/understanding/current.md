# CURRENT: How Fitzpatrick's teaching changed, and which version a skill should encode

Source base: the ten deep-read reports (read_01 to read_10, covering V1 to V50), with the main reversals checked against the transcripts in `readable/`. Dates are publication dates. Citations are V-number plus m:ss. Everything is paraphrase. His coined terms appear as names, not quotations. Double quotation marks mark short labels or slogans; most are paraphrased, and none of the few verbatim ones runs past a handful of words. Examples are my own illustrations, not his.

Change labels used below:
- **Renamed**: same idea, new name or image.
- **Expanded**: same idea, more steps, cases or scales.
- **Compressed**: fewer steps, or a step folded into another.
- **Merged**: two separate ideas became one.
- **Qualified**: a limit, exception or priority rule was added.
- **Reversed**: he now says the opposite, or explicitly withdraws the earlier advice.
- **Open**: he leaves a live tension unresolved; the skill has to pick a rule.

How "current" was chosen for each item:
1. If he explicitly says he changed his mind, the old version is never encoded.
2. Otherwise the latest explicit statement wins, unless it is a one-video emphasis that contradicts his own mature premise or practice. In that case I say so and pick the version that fits the mature system.
3. Earlier details that the later version does not contradict are kept as sub-rules.
4. Topics taught once and never revisited (dialogue, book types, literature reviews) are encoded as dated modules with a caveat, not as core doctrine.

## Part 2. The eras of the channel

The 50 long-form videos fall into six eras, separated mostly by publishing gaps. Each era changes the audience, the kind of evidence he offers, and the unit of writing he cares most about.

### Era 1. The academic writing instructor (Feb to May 2024, V1 to V5)

- **Who he is on camera:** a working English professor. He mentions the university writing center where he teaches (V3 1:47).
- **Audience:** graduate students, first-year undergraduates, researchers. Topics are literature reviews, grammar pedantry, freshman mistakes and citation counts.
- **What he teaches:** synthesize rather than list; cast the literature as major and minor characters; the claim must be significant, non-obvious and debatable; hedges and boosters as calibration; personal language in the abstract and introduction but not in methods; present tense for your findings; judge grammar by its effect on the reader.
- **How he argues:** from experience, writing-center practice and peer-review comments, then (V5) a corpus of 30,000 articles and the Oppenheimer study.
- **Relationship to school:** university writing is the target to master; high school is what misled you (V3 3:21).
- **AI:** mentioned once. AI produces language, not judgment (V1 10:40).
- **What survives:** the reader-effect test (V2 0:21), analysis over description (V3), the debatable claim (V3 8:00), plot versus story and casting (V4) as the ancestor of "cut what doesn't serve," the end of the sentence as prime real estate (V2 9:38), content words versus function words (V5 4:43).
- **What is superseded:** the "missing piece of the puzzle" framing of a contribution (V4 5:47), reversed by V31. The proverb about the watch is used in the opposite direction from later videos (see Part 3).

Gap: May to August 2024.

### Era 2. Reader-psychology craft tools for any writer (Aug 2024 to May 2025, V6 to V15)

- **Who he is on camera:** still presented as teaching at a Korean university (V11, V12). By V14 (May 2025) he speaks of his professorship in the past tense (V14 0:00).
- **Audience:** "any writer," including fiction writers. V12 and V13 draw heavily on novels, film and poetry.
- **What he teaches:** sentence-level clarity from reader psychology. Short subject and early verb (V8 tunnel, V12 locomotive); zombie nouns; old to new (V8, V9 relay race, V15 battery and handshake); two skis of sentence and passage topic (V9); concreteness and memory (V9); metadiscourse replaced by conversational moves (V9); great sentences as successful sentences (V10); non-editing and working with words and ideas (V11); 31 rhetorical tools (V12); silent dialogue and ellipsis (V13); the five uses of the passive and the stress position (V14); flow and the passive conversion (V15).
- **How he argues:** live demonstrations ("which sentence is easier?"), memory exercises, literary examples, and the rules-versus-tools frame.
- **Format:** "hacks," numbered tools, one vivid analogy per concept.
- **AI:** first appears as a source of bad rules in grammar checkers (V14 7:44) and bad habits (V15 0:00).
- **What survives:** nearly all the sentence mechanics, renamed and systematized later. The passive-voice rehabilitation starts here.
- **What is superseded:** the passive "maybe 5%" quota (V6 11:47, V15 13:34), the unqualified "don't edit while drafting" (V11), which he later calls vague (V19 12:45), and the idea that the Bilbo-moment story opening is the default (V12 41:39).

Gap: June 2025.

### Era 3. The expert-writer turn: value, books and the two-draft system (Jul to Oct 2025, V16 to V25)

- **Who he is on camera:** a coach and editor with a consulting practice. He repositions the channel from "writers" to "expert writers" and accepts losing subscribers (V16 8:58 to 10:24).
- **Audience:** experts, consultants, founders, academics writing for outsiders, book authors.
- **What he teaches:** book positioning (3Vs, six book types, the Dan Koe post-mortem); the 27-step expert writing system and the two-draft process (V19); the two-player game and the template trap (V20); creative nonfiction craft (V21); problem construction, topic string, code words and intuition pumps from the Arthur and McCarthy article (V22); writer's block as fear, with an explicit habit-and-discipline regime (V23); the editor's seven-step top-down diagnostic and the coffee stain problem (V24); the reader's definition of the essay (V25).
- **Key shifts inside the era:** value before clarity (V19 8:34); writer patterns versus reader patterns and the first use of interference (V19 25:11, V24 4:18); school becomes the main villain (V19, V20, with an apology on behalf of English professors, V20 11:11); persuasion gives way to understanding as the essay's function (V22 22:52 versus V25 2:24).
- **AI:** the channel's named villain is AI slop (V16 13:10). V19 opposes AI for writing and research. V20 still allows AI to tidy a voice transcript (V20 7:40).
- **What survives:** the value engine, the reader brief, interference, the top-down editing order, code words, the essay definition.
- **What is superseded:** the discipline regime of V23; the absolute "blank document, no copy and paste" of V19 28:15 (softened by V50); "be confident, be strident" (V19 1:05:09); "nonfiction is persuasion" (V19, V20, V22).

Transition: V26 (26 Oct 2025) opens Era 4, then there is a gap until V27 (4 Jan 2026).

### Era 4. Principles and diagnostics (Oct 2025 to Feb 2026, V26 to V35)

- **Who he is on camera:** a writing teacher codifying a system. Many videos debunk a named piece of "worst advice."
- **Audience:** academics and professionals: grant applicants, PhD applicants, founders, newsletter writers.
- **What he teaches:** the mental briefcase and working memory of three or four items (V26); nine techniques at sentence, paragraph and idea level, index and discussion at every scale, four signals of coherence (V27); the three-part introduction from 100 openings (V28); compression (V29); joint attention and the rhetorical planning wheel (V30); five school habits including the martini glass and the gap model (V31); strategic repetition and the movie-poster test (V32); choosing the subject by what the reader cares about (V33); the em dash as an emphasis tool (V34); thinking on paper, reverse outline and directions to the restaurant (V35).
- **How he argues:** close revisions of model passages; the structure closely tracks the Williams/Gopen tradition, though he names no source.
- **AI:** moves from background to frame. AI clean-up loses your voice (V27 0:00); rules plus AI tools stop writers thinking about readers (V33 13:04); the em-dash panic is a symptom, the hollow confident voice is the disease (V34 12:17).
- **What survives:** almost everything; this era supplies most of the sentence and paragraph diagnostics the mature system uses.
- **What is superseded or narrowed:** the five-move introduction (compressed by V28 within a week); "curse of knowledge" as a label (V27 33:17, rejected by V47 13:39); heavy reliance on synonyms for key terms (narrowed by V48).

Gap: 22 February to 1 April 2026.

### Era 5. The human edge and the inner game (Apr to May 2026, V36 to V43)

- **Who he is on camera:** more philosophical and more openly self-revising. The headline video is "Writing Advice I've Changed My Mind About" (V42).
- **Audience:** experts and online writers worried about AI and about block.
- **What he teaches:** five human skills AI cannot replace (voice, embodied metaphor, deliberate rule-breaking, stakes, whimsy) (V36); a full Williams-style paragraph revision with topic/comment and index position (V37); the inquiry method and style as integrity (V38); storytelling moves in science writing: one question, foreground reporting, time markers, setup and payoff, building a bridge (V39); a Zettelkasten with AI as read-only navigation (V40); giving up discipline, the unconscious, Lacan's three registers, goal-free freewriting (V41); five reversals (V42); the impersonal essay and six arrows (V43).
- **How he argues:** psychoanalysis, cognitive linguistics, philosophy of science, and his own history.
- **AI:** allowed as a navigation layer and, in V40, to clean up a voice-memo transcript for a private fleeting note (V40 3:00). Not for thinking or writing.
- **What survives:** the two-phase architecture in its clearest form (V38 phase-skip diagnostic; V42 artist and architect), the reader-change test (V42 23:06), the inquiry method, the impersonal turn for essays.
- **What is superseded:** the discipline frame (V23) and outline-first (V17, V18); the strong claim that language and thought are inseparable (V38 21:34), softened by V40 and V42.

Gap: 21 May to 20 July 2026 (he ran a two-month writing cohort).

### Era 6. Community and the reader-location system (Jul to Sep 2026, V44 to V50)

- **Who he is on camera:** a cohort leader and editor who once ran a university writing lab for seven years (V47 0:00).
- **Audience:** independent writers, experts, grant writers; explicitly limited to pointed writing (V50 0:48).
- **What he teaches:** early readers and a community of practice, the letter of response, the silent author (V44); the hard line on AI (V45); five reader locations: title, introduction, index, key terms, conclusion (V46); interference and the second document (V47); four topic-progression patterns (V48); private, contextual and textual structure, coherence is not in the text (V49); the six-part messy-draft system: frame, point placement, section indexes, point anatomy, uneven U, calibration (V50).
- **How he argues:** research on readers (the Carnegie Mellon briefed-reader study, V50 2:00; research on intrigue, V46 2:16; spatial navigation in reading, V42 19:27), plus client and cohort cases.
- **AI:** no AI in communicative writing at any stage (V45); AI allowed as navigation and, narrowly, to find a better verb (V50 34:15).
- **Status:** this era is the mature synthesis. It restates earlier tools as one premise (the reader builds coherence) and one method (put the right content in the locations readers look).

### Cross-era arcs

1. **From rules, to the reader's mind, to the reader relationship.** 2024 judges by reader processing (V2). 2025 builds tools on memory and attention (V9, V26). 2026 adds that readers co-create meaning (V33 8:53, V49) and that voice and block depend on a real relationship with readers (V44, V45).
2. **From the sentence up to the document.** Era 2 works at sentence level. Era 3 adds value and whole-book structure. Era 4 connects scales through index and discussion. Era 6 anchors everything to five reader locations.
3. **From students to experts, then to "pointed writing."** The audience narrows on purpose (V16) and the method's scope is finally stated (V50 0:48).
4. **From many steps to few.** 27 steps (V19, Aug 2025), 7 steps (V24, Sep 2025), 9 techniques (V27, Jan 2026), 5 locations (V46, Jul 2026), a 6-part procedure (V50, Sep 2026). Each later packaging is tighter and more location-based.
5. **School: from the fix to the villain.** In V3 university argument is the model. From V18 onward, all school writing, including the university essay, is built for a paid reader (V18 8:24, V20 9:33, V31, V46 5:13).
6. **AI: from a footnote to a boundary.** One mention (V1), a source of bad rules (V14), the channel's villain (V16), a partial tool (V20, V40, V44), then a hard line for communicative writing (V45).
7. **Self-revision in public.** He admits vague early advice (V19 11:50), teaching the school habits himself (V31 0:00), the failed discipline regime (V41 1:13), and five withdrawn positions (V42).

## Part 3. A telling reversal in miniature: the watch

The same proverb flips direction, and the flip shows the whole arc.
- **V3 2:32 (Mar 2024):** he reverses the proverb for university students. When asked about a watch, you should explain how it works; description is not enough.
- **V12 46:41 (Feb 2025):** the watch illustrates analysis over description. Same direction.
- **V28 12:20 (Jan 2026):** the proverb is used straight. A long background lecture before your solution is like answering "what time is it?" with how to build a watch.

For a skill: keep "analysis over description" (V3, V12) and "no background lecture before the solution" (V28). They apply to different places: the first to the body, the second to the introduction.

## Part 4. Framework-by-framework traces

### A. Process

#### A1. The two phases (renamed four times, then made more practical)
- **Versions:**
  - V11 2:03 to 2:24 (Jan 2025): door closed for writing, door open for rewriting. Drafting is for you; rewriting is for the reader.
  - V19 21:25 to 29:54 (Aug 2025): the two-draft system. A terrible first draft to think, then a complete rewrite in a new document; no copy and paste (28:15). Writer patterns interfere with reader patterns (25:11).
  - V20 7:40 to 9:33 (Aug 2025): the writer's draft goes in the drawer; start a new reader's draft.
  - V23 35:22 (Sep 2025): artist and architect named as two modes.
  - V24 4:18 to 7:28 (Sep 2025): writing patterns versus reading patterns; interference.
  - V31 7:55 to 10:11 (Jan 2026): two functions, two phases, two documents.
  - V35 9:41 to 13:27 (Feb 2026): directions to the restaurant; you cannot line-edit a thinking draft into a reader draft.
  - V38 16:38 to 21:34 (Apr 2026): phase 1 and phase 2, with the phase-skip diagnostic (skip phase 2 and nobody reads; skip phase 1 and you get audience capture).
  - V42 16:31 to 20:01 (May 2026): artist phase (timed, for no reader) and architect phase (find the structure native to the material).
  - V47 9:18 to 18:15 (Aug 2026): interference is the main problem of expert writing; the reader draft is a second document.
  - V50 0:00, 15:06 (Sep 2026): extract the points from the messy draft, relocate them, rebuild around them, preserving meaning and voice.
- **Change:** renamed (door, draft, artist/architect, phase, generation/adaptation); expanded (the phase-skip diagnostic, the reason: interference); qualified at the end (V50 works from the old draft's material rather than a blank page).
- **Encode:** Phase 1 generates knowledge for the writer; phase 2 builds a reader document. The move between them is structural: find the points, put them in reader locations, reorder by reader questions. Reusing sentences from the thinking draft is fine when they now sit in the reader's order; polishing the thinking draft in its own order is not.
- **Why:** V50 is the latest and most operational version and is compatible with V47 (V50 is structural rewriting, not line editing). V19's "no copy and paste" was the most absolute form; he never repeats it after V20.

#### A2. When to revise: separated or recursive? (reversed, then reconciled)
- **Versions:**
  - V11 2:24, 5:13 (Jan 2025): don't edit while producing; schedule editing separately.
  - V19 12:45 (Aug 2025): he cites his own early "don't edit while you're writing" as an example of vague, indiscriminate advice.
  - V23 36:18 to 38:09 (Sep 2025): separating writing from revision is backwards. Real writing is recursive: pause after each section, reread, map the whole, find the best idea and build around it. Revision is where you discover what you mean.
  - V23 34:19 (Sep 2025): but concision comes late ("writing anorexia" if you prune early).
  - V41 27:53 (May 2026): you cannot sculpt magma; generating and judging at the same moment blocks flow.
  - V47 21:09 (Aug 2026): don't think about reading patterns while generating.
- **Change:** reversed (V11 to V23), then reconciled.
- **Encode:** Separate two things. (1) Judging sentences while generating them: avoid it (V11, V41). (2) Rereading what you have written to see its shape and find the strongest idea: encouraged during drafting (V23). What must never mix is reader orientation into the generation phase (V47). Cutting for concision waits until you are rich in material (V23).
- **Why:** V23 explicitly rejects the time-sliced model; V41 and V47 explicitly reject judging in the moment of generation. Both hold at once when "revision" means structural rereading and "editing" means in-the-moment judgment.

#### A3. Outlining (reversed)
- **Versions:**
  - V17 38:16 (Jul 2025): if you want to write a book, hold off writing until you have a blueprint.
  - V18 17:41, 23:07 (Jul 2025): exploring by "pantsing" is fine, but compiling a book needs a table of ideas per chapter.
  - V23 23:19 (Sep 2025): when you lose faith, write an outline of what you actually have.
  - V35 8:11 to 9:41 (Feb 2026): the reverse outline replaces the planned outline; label what each section does.
  - V42 14:20 to 20:01 (May 2026): he admits he preached and practised outline-first and that it gave him block. If you need an outline, make it after the first draft; it is already in there.
  - V47 19:13 to 22:07 (Aug 2026): the skim test is a reverse outline; outline-first does not fit expert thinking.
  - V43 4:56 to 8:50 (May 2026): the six arrows, with the first and last lines written first (see C9 for the tension).
- **Change:** reversed (explicitly in V42).
- **Encode:** No outline before the thinking draft. After it, run the reverse outline: mark sections, label what each does, read the labels alone, check they follow the reader's questions. A planned structure (table of contents, six arrows) is allowed only once the writer knows their point.
- **Why:** V42 is an explicit change of mind; V47 repeats it. V17 and V18 were about the book-compiling stage, which the current rule still permits after the thinking is done.

#### A4. Freewriting (expanded into two distinct modes)

## Part 5. Tensions the skill must settle by rule

1. **Readers as feedback (V44 vs V47).** Rule: readers locate, the writer diagnoses (A8).
2. **AI use (V40, V44 vs V45).** Rule: nothing AI-written or AI-polished for readers; navigation, logistics, private aids and word lookup only (A9).
3. **Fixed forms versus emergent structure (V42 vs V43).** Rule: fixed forms are phase-2 contextual structures, used after the point is known (C9).
4. **Complete rewrite versus reuse (V19 vs V50).** Rule: structural rebuild in reader order; reuse material when it now sits where the reader needs it (A1).
5. **Writing is thinking versus the night shift (V41 vs V47).** Rule: the page is where the untangling happens; incubation feeds it (A6).
6. **Hedged versus bold (V17, V19 vs V18, V50).** Rule: calibrate by the reader community and earn strength with reasons (B7).
7. **Persuade versus inform (V19, V22 vs V25).** Rule: understanding by default; persuasion for persuasive genres (B2).
8. **Repetition versus synonyms (V46 vs V48).** Rule: synonyms continue topics but never rename key terms (D7).
9. **Signposts (V26 "no signposts" vs V27, V37, V39 signals).** Rule: content signals and navigation markers stay; announcements that patch a private order go (D8).
10. **Character subject versus old-first (V6/V27 vs V37).** Rule: old-first wins in conflict, unless agency matters to the reader (D2).
